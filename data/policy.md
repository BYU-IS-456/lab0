# Summit Ridge Outfitters — Purchasing and Accounts Payable Policy

Owner: Controller. Applies to all purchases of goods and services and all vendor invoices.

## Purpose and scope

This policy explains how Summit Ridge Outfitters buys goods and services and how accounts payable decides whether a vendor invoice can be paid. It applies to every department and every vendor. Where this policy and a signed vendor contract disagree, the contract controls the price and the policy controls the process.

## Purchase orders

A purchase order (PO) is required before ordering any goods or services over $2,500. The PO must name the vendor, describe what is being bought, and state the total approved amount.

Recurring charges under a signed contract, such as warehouse rent and utilities, are paid against the contract and do not need a PO. Any other invoice over $2,500 that arrives without a PO is placed on hold until the requesting department obtains an approved PO.

## Approval limits

Purchase orders must be approved before the order is placed, by someone with enough authority for the amount:

- Up to $5,000: a department manager.
- Over $5,000 and up to $25,000: a director.
- Over $25,000: the CFO.

A purchase may not be split into smaller POs to stay under an approval limit.

## New vendors

A vendor must be approved before we issue it a PO or pay any of its invoices. Approval requires a completed W-9, verified bank details, and a check that the vendor is not on a sanctions list. Until approval is complete, the vendor's status is pending approval.

Invoices from a vendor with pending approval are placed on hold. Accounts payable notifies purchasing, which completes the vendor setup before the invoice can be released.

## Inactive vendors

A vendor is marked inactive when we stop doing business with it or it has not been used for 18 months. No new POs may be issued to an inactive vendor, and invoices from an inactive vendor are not paid automatically. They are placed on hold and sent to the accounts payable manager, who confirms whether the goods or services were actually ordered and received.

## Three-way match

Before an invoice is paid, accounts payable matches three documents:

1. The purchase order: what we agreed to buy, and the approved amount.
2. The receiving record: what we actually received.
3. The vendor's invoice: what the vendor is asking us to pay.

An invoice is paid only when all three agree within the tolerance in the next section. If there is no receiving record yet, the invoice waits on hold until the goods arrive. We never pay for more than we have received.

## Price and quantity variances

Small differences between the invoice and the PO are allowed, so that freight and rounding don't stop every payment. An invoice may be paid if it exceeds the PO amount by no more than 5% of the PO amount or $500, whichever is smaller.

An invoice that exceeds the PO by more than that tolerance is placed on hold. To release it, either the buyer amends the PO and has the amendment approved at the right approval limit, or the vendor issues a corrected invoice or credit memo.

## Duplicate invoices

Paying the same invoice twice is one of the most common and costly accounts payable errors. Before an invoice is paid, accounts payable checks for duplicates.

An invoice is treated as a likely duplicate if it has the same vendor and the same vendor invoice number as an invoice already received, or the same vendor and the same amount as another invoice within 30 days with a vendor invoice number that differs only in punctuation, spaces or leading zeros (for example, "BC-4471" and "BC4471").

Regular recurring charges, such as monthly rent, have the same amount every month but a different vendor invoice number each time. They are not duplicates.

A likely duplicate is placed on hold until the vendor confirms in writing that it is a separate charge. If a duplicate has already been paid, accounts payable requests a refund or a credit against the vendor's next invoice.

## Payment holds

An invoice on hold is not paid until the reason for the hold is resolved. Common reasons are:

- no approved PO when one is required,
- no receiving record, or less received than invoiced,
- an amount over the PO beyond the variance tolerance,
- a likely duplicate,
- a vendor that is inactive or pending approval.

The accounts payable specialist records the reason for the hold. Only the accounts payable manager or the controller may release an invoice from hold, and the release must state how the reason was resolved.

## Payment terms and discounts

Invoices are paid on their due date, which is set by the vendor's payment terms (for example, Net 30 means 30 days after the invoice date). When a vendor offers an early-payment discount, such as 2/10 Net 30 (2% off if paid within 10 days), accounts payable takes the discount whenever the invoice is approved in time. Otherwise invoices are not paid early.

## Changes to vendor bank details

Requests to change a vendor's bank account are a common fraud method. Any change must be confirmed by calling the vendor at a phone number already on file, never a number given in the request itself. Payments to the vendor are paused until the change is confirmed.

## Record retention

Purchase orders, receiving records, invoices and payment records are kept for seven years after the end of the fiscal year in which payment was made.
